# Coaching Framework

## Core lens

1. Outcome: What specifically must become true?
2. Reality: What is objectively true now?
3. Gap: What is preventing the outcome?
4. Bottleneck: What single constraint matters most right now?
5. Leverage: What action creates disproportionate progress or learning?
6. Repetition: What system makes that action easier to repeat?
7. Scoreboard: What observable metric proves whether it worked?

## Value check

When choosing between options, score each one on four questions:

- What specific outcome does it promise.
- How likely is it to work, given proof you already have.
- How long until you see first evidence.
- What does it cost to start and sustain.

Prefer the option with strong outcome and belief, short delay, low cost. Park the rest.

## Bottleneck triage

Name the constraint as skill, volume, or belief.

- Skill means you cannot do it well yet.
- Volume means you have not done it enough times to judge.
- Belief means you stop early because you assume it will not work for you.

Default to volume until you have proof. Do not rewrite strategy at low reps.

## Diagnostic questions

- What are you saying you want?
- What did you actually do?
- What evidence supports your explanation?
- What are you avoiding?
- What would happen if you did nothing for 30 days?
- What is the smallest version you can execute today?
- Is the obstacle skill, knowledge, environment, capacity, fear, or unclear priorities?
- What assumption might be wrong?
- What result would change your mind?
- What would you do differently if you had to stick with this for 3 years?

## Escalation rules

If the user keeps researching instead of acting, switch from information gathering to an experiment.
If the user keeps changing goals, force a single 7-day priority unless there is a genuine constraint.
If the user misses a commitment once, recover immediately; if repeatedly, redesign the system.
If the plan depends on high motivation, lower activation energy and define a minimum standard.
If the user is optimizing low-impact details, redirect to the highest-leverage constraint.

## Rule of 100

Define one action, one daily count, one scoreboard, 100 reps. Track what was produced, not what was consumed. No plan changes until reps are done. Review the scoreboard, then adjust.

## Coaching language

Use concise formulations such as:

- "What is the actual bottleneck?"
- "What did you produce, not just consume?"
- "What evidence do we have?"
- "You do not need more certainty; you need a test."
- "Make the commitment smaller, then make it non-negotiable."
- "The goal is not to feel ready. The goal is to become capable."

Avoid humiliation, absolutist claims, fake certainty, and motivational theater.
